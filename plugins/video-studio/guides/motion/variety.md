# The Variety standard

The review's **Variety** score is judged against this page; the script and storyboard plan to it, and the build
keeps to the plan. The interval numbers are in `../numbers.md`.

## What good looks like

- **Something new every few seconds.** A new element, a new framing, a reveal, a camera move, a cut: the eye always
  has a reason to stay, and nothing sits dead while the voiceover talks (`../numbers.md`, "Something new").
- **No device used twice.** Each beat says its idea with its own picture and its own move. When similar things
  repeat (three features, four steps), each gets its own layout or treatment, or one deliberate repeating frame
  (`choreography.md`, "Chapters") whose content changes, never the same card three times by accident.
- **Variety inside the film's personality.** The brand's eases, durations and colours stay constant; what changes is
  the framing, the scale, the direction, the rhythm and the kind of move.
- **Contrast of pace.** Fast stretches and held moments, big and small, busy and still: the slowest scene is about
  three times slower than the fastest.
- **Several kinds of transition, each with a reason** (`transitions.md`): a film uses a few, chosen for what each
  seam does, never one for every cut, and never one at random.

## How to get there

- **Script:** give each row a different action on screen; when a list or set of reasons comes up, say how each one
  is shown differently, or that one frame repeats on purpose.
- **Storyboard:** for each scene, note its framing, the move it uses and how it leaves, and look down the list: no
  two scenes in a row enter the same way, scale the same way or sit on the same layout. Vary the eases and
  staggers scene by scene, and the ambient treatment (a slow pan, a colour shift, nothing) rather than one ambient
  zoom on every scene.
- **Build:** when a scene runs long, add a beat (a new reveal, a push to a detail, a cut to a new angle), never a
  slower tween; any single treatment goes stale after about 6 seconds.

## What lowers it

- Every element fading and sliding up; every scene entering the same way; the same move beat after beat.
- Three cards in a row, a grid of figures, a title over a dimmed screenshot standing in for a scene.
- The same transition on every cut; the same sound on every cut (`../audio/sfx.md`).
- Long holds with nothing changing; or the opposite, a frantic film with no still moments.

## How it's scored

| Score | Means |
|---|---|
| 9–10 | Something new on every beat, every idea shown its own way, pace that rises and falls, transitions that each have a reason |
| 8 | Varied, with one repeat or one flat stretch |
| 6–7 | Noticeable repetition: a device reused, a stretch with nothing new, one transition doing most of the work |
| 4–5 | Mostly the same move or layout throughout, or long dead stretches |
| 1–3 | A slideshow: one treatment for the whole film |
