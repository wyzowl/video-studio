# Explainers

A film that makes an idea, a process or a product understood, usually with pictures invented for it: type, diagrams,
shapes and data rather than footage. An article is an information dump; an explainer is a guided act of
understanding.

## Find the teaching truth

From the brief and material, name:
- **The audience,** and what they already know and don't.
- **The gap:** the confusion, question or "why care" the film resolves.
- **The thesis:** the one line the viewer walks away with.
- **The spine:** the three to six ideas (steps, mechanisms, items) that build to it.
- **The evidence:** the real numbers, examples and comparisons that ground it.
- **The landing:** the takeaway, or what to try or do.

Write the film around the thesis. Never paraphrase the source in its own order: reorder, merge, leave out, and keep
the spine.

## Choose one structure

| Structure | What it is | Use for | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The concept** | what is it, and why does it matter | one idea the audience half knows | name it, reveal the mechanism layer by layer, land what it means |
| **The how-to** | how to do it, or how it works, in order | a process with a clear start and finish | three to six steps on one consistent stage, one move each |
| **The list** | N things about it | parallel, equal items (tips, mistakes, reasons) | the hook, the items (three is strongest), a wrap |
| **The story** | teach through what happened | a case, a history, a cautionary tale | set-up, tension, the turn, the resolution, the lesson |

Choose one, or name a combination (a concept with its steps inside). Don't splice phases from different ones.

## The body is a sequence

The core of an explainer is three to six beats on a consistent stage, each adding one layer, step or item, building
understanding as it goes; a single isolated beat rarely teaches anything. Carry the object being explained from beat
to beat (`../beats.md`, "Runs of beats"), and use one kind of transition through the run.

## Inventing the pictures

With nothing to film, the hero of each beat is designed:
- **Type as the subject:** the coined term, the key number, a short enumeration, set big. In many explainers type is
  the main visual.
- **Shapes that are the idea:** the snowball, the spotlight, the staircase rather than the cliff: build the metaphor
  the script names, rather than decorating with generic shapes.
- **Diagrams and data:** nodes and connections, a chart, a number line, a flow, assembling part by part as it's
  explained; the build is the teaching.

The designed hero fills 40–60% of the frame (a diagram big enough for its labels to read); a real image the user
supplies takes the hero's place or the ground.

## Explaining a product

When the subject is a product, the same structures apply with the product's real interface at the "how it works"
beats (`../../design/brand.md`, "Real assets"), and the visual approach chosen from the story (`../principles.md`,
"How to get there", step 3).
