# Hooks, and the Hook standard

The review's **Hook** score is judged against this page: whether the first seconds earn the rest of the film. The
script writes its openings from it, the storyboard designs the opening frames to it, and the review scores the same
thing. The times are in `../numbers.md`, "The hook and the end".

## The Hook standard

### What good looks like

- **It grabs at once.** The first frame is already a composed, meaningful frame, and something moves or reads within
  the first second or so; the promise is clear within 3 seconds (1.5 in a vertical feed).
- **Tension, curiosity or desire.** The opening opens a gap the viewer wants closed: a problem they feel, a question
  they can't answer, a claim that surprises, a future they want.
- **In the viewer's language:** what they gain, avoid or finally understand. Never the company introducing itself,
  a generic definition, or the product's own vocabulary.
- **A promise the film keeps.** The opening's problem or question is resolved by the end (`principles.md`).
- **Words and picture together.** The opening line and the opening picture make the same move: the picture shows the
  problem the voice names, or the word swaps as the voice says it.
- **Fits the film:** a calm, considered opening for a premium film; a punch for a launch; a question for an
  explainer. Not every film should open on a statistic.

### How to get there

- **Script:** write two or three openings from different patterns below, use the strongest, and give the others in
  the script's summary so the user can swap. Lead the first row with the hook; never "Welcome to…" or
  "Introducing…" as the first words.
- **Storyboard:** design the first frame as a still that already says something; plan what moves in the first second.
- **Build:** no fade up from black, no empty opening frame, no logo first unless the logo is the hook.
- **Review:** watch the first 3 seconds cold, as someone scrolling past would.

### What lowers it

| What you see | Usual cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A slow start | a fade from black, a logo sting, scene-setting before anything happens | start on the hook's first frame |
| "Welcome to…" | the company introducing itself | the viewer's problem or desire first |
| A definition | "X is a platform that…" | a question, a problem or a picture first; the definition later |
| The same opening as every film | a statistic by reflex | choose the pattern from the film's message |
| A promise not kept | a hook unrelated to the message | a hook the ending pays off |
| Words and picture apart | a generic picture under a strong line | the picture making the line's move |

### How it's scored

| Score | Means |
|---|---|
| 9–10 | The first second stops a scroll; the promise is clear by 3 s and the film pays it off |
| 8 | Strong and clear; it could land a beat sooner or harder |
| 6–7 | Clear but ordinary: a familiar pattern, or a slow first second |
| 4–5 | Slow, generic or about the company; the promise arrives late |
| 1–3 | No hook: black, a logo or a title card, then the film starts |

## Openings

Our names for the openings a script can choose from. Each works through the words, the picture, or both.

### Through the words

| Opening | How it works | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **The Itch** | names a frustration the viewer already feels | "Every Monday, the same spreadsheet." |
| **The Turn** | says the world has changed | "The way teams hire has changed." |
| **The Open Question** | asks something the film will answer | "Why do good projects still run late?" |
| **The Upside-down** | contradicts what people believe | "Fewer meetings made us slower." |
| **The Number** | one figure with stakes, from the brief (sparingly) | "Nine in ten…" |
| **The Picture** | makes an abstract idea physical | "Your inbox is a tap nobody turns off." |
| **The Name** | lands a term worth knowing | "There's a word for this." |
| **The Call-out** | speaks to exactly who it's for | "If you've ever lost a day to a broken build…" |
| **The What-if** | imagines a better way | "Imagine reports that write themselves overnight." |
| **The Meet** | introduces a person whose story this is | "Meet Sam. Sam runs a bakery." |
| **The Cost** | shows what it costs to ignore | "Miss this step and the whole batch is wasted." |
| **The Before-and-after** | the whole change in one breath | "One line crashed. Now it recovers." |

### Through the picture

| Opening | How it works | Scene pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **The Swap** | a punchy line whose key word swaps or escalates in place | `words-in-beats` |
| **The Typed Thought** | an everyday thought typed and corrected live | `typed-line` |
| **The Timeline** | milestones march past, landing on "until now" | `pan-across-stations` |
| **The Pile-up** | tools and tabs pile in until they bury the viewer | `closing-in` |
| **The Shortlist** | "Is it this? Or this?" cycles, then the answer crashes in | `takeover` |
| **The Ask** | a request types into the product; the question is the hook | `prompt-and-answer` |
| **The Mystery** | an unreadable close-up that makes sense as the camera pulls back | `zoom-out-reveal` |
| **The Roll-call** | one line holds while who it's for cycles beneath it | `fixed-anchor` |
| **The Busy Room** | a workspace already alive with people at work | `cursor-demo` |
| **The Cold Number** | one figure counting up, nothing else on screen | `data-hero` |
| **The Little Trick** | one small thing on a bare field transforms | `mark-to-button` |

The scene patterns are in `../motion/patterns/`.

### By type of video

| Video | Openings that suit it |
|---|---|
| Explainer | The Open Question, The Upside-down, The Picture, The Name, The Itch, The Cost, The What-if |
| Launch or promo | The Itch, The Swap, The Shortlist, The What-if, The Call-out, The Pile-up, The Cold Number |
| Product demo | The Ask, The Busy Room, The Little Trick, The Mystery, The Typed Thought |
| Brand story | The Meet, The Turn, The Picture, The What-if |
| Code change or release | The Before-and-after, The Itch (the bug everyone hits), The Name, The Number (only with stakes) |
| Talking head, interview, talk | The Cold Open: the speaker's strongest line first, then into the story |
| Cut-downs | The Cold Open on the clip's best line; never a greeting (`types/cut-downs.md`) |
| Music-led | the picture's first hit on the first strong beat; The Swap or The Cold Number in type |
| Vertical social | anything above, with words on screen from the first frame and movement in the first second |
