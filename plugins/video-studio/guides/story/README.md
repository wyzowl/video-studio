## The spine, for every narrated film

- **Value first.** The opening speaks the viewer's language: what they gain, avoid or finally understand, never the
  product's own vocabulary (feature names, file names, a page's headings). The film's one message lands by the
  second beat; everything after it is evidence for that message. Test it: remove the evidence beats and the message
  still stands; remove the message beats and the film falls apart into a tour.
- **The plan is a proposal.** The script and storyboard open with one line, "This video tells [audience] that
  [message]", and every beat's job traces back to that message; a beat whose job doesn't is cut, not decorated.
- **Pictures come from the source.** Mine the brief, the product and the material for the film's props: its own
  words, numbers, objects and metaphors. If a prop could appear unchanged in another company's video, it didn't come
  from the source: swap it for something only this subject could produce.
- **The input isn't the outline.** A web page, an article or a brief is information, not a story: reorder, merge,
  compress and leave out. Following the source's order is the most common way a film goes flat.

A film led by music, a deck, or footage whose story is fixed (a talk, an interview) follows its own shape: see
`types/`.
