# Narration and words

Writing the voiceover, the words on screen, and any words that go out with the video. The pace and word counts are in
`../numbers.md`; the script's format is the script skill's.

## For the ear

- **Talk to one person,** as "you". Warm and direct, never corporate.
- **Contractions and natural rhythm:** "it's", "you'll", "that's"; short punchy lines mixed with longer flowing
  ones; the odd question ("right?").
- **One or two sentences a beat,** usually 6–20 words, written as cues: clear phrase breaks give the picture a
  moment to reveal each piece as it's named ("First the draft, then the check, then it's sent"). A long single
  clause leaves the picture nothing to land on.
- **Carry a sentence across beats** with ellipses, breaking at natural pauses ("Book a desk…", "…from anywhere…",
  "…in seconds."), so the picture changes while the voice flows on. It's the main pacing device.
- **Lead with what the viewer gets.** Name things the way the viewer would, not the way the product is built: you
  "turn off notifications", not "configure webhooks".
- **Shorter than the video.** Leave room for pauses and for the picture to breathe; silence between sentences is a
  feature. A beat can be silent when the picture proves the point (a diagram assembling, a demo playing).
- **Numbers as they're said.** Write what the reader should say ("nearly two million", "ten times", "A P I") when a
  figure or a word could trip them; the screen can show the exact figure while the voice rounds it.
- **Read it aloud.** If it sounds stiff, rewrite it.

## When the user brings the words

- **Their words, kept:** split the script into beats at sentence or clause breaks without changing a word; the
  length follows their script.
- **Their words, reshaped:** when they ask for it, treat their script as material: reorder, merge, cut and rewrite to
  fit the shape and the length, and say what changed.
- A recorded voiceover is fixed: its words can't change without a new recording, so the picture fits the words.

## Words that sound like a person

For voiceovers, words on screen, and the words that go out with the video (`post-words.md`):
- **Plain verbs and specific nouns.** "Sends the invoice" rather than "facilitates invoicing".
- **Stock phrases out:** "unlock the power of", "seamless experience", "streamline your workflow", "take it to the
  next level", "game-changer", "revolutionise", "elevate", "in today's fast-paced world", "whether you're a… or a…",
  "say goodbye to…", "look no further". Each says nothing this product could own.
- **No stacked adjectives or triplets by reflex** ("fast, simple and powerful"); one true word beats three.
- **No hedging or filler:** "really", "very", "just", "simply", "actually" only when they do work.
- **Vary the rhythm:** not every sentence the same length, not every line a fragment.
- **No borrowed drama:** rhetorical questions one after another, "Here's the thing:", "The best part?", "Let that
  sink in."
- **Punctuation like a person:** full stops and commas; dashes and exclamation marks rarely.
- **Say it the way you'd say it to a colleague,** then cut a third.

## Words on screen

- A few key words, not the voiceover's sentence: they echo or sharpen what's said, and arrive as it's said
  (`../numbers.md`, "Speech").
- In a film without a voiceover, the words on screen are the script: short lines, one idea each, timed for reading.
- Every word on screen is marked in the script (bold), so the build knows what becomes text.
